## Items requiring confirmation

These are independent variants. Do not combine their concentrations and times. KOH, HCl and staining times include alternatives or incomplete edits. The English description omits glycerol and Trypan blue quantities. An informal personal attribution was replaced with a descriptive heading; experimental values were retained.

## Variant 1 — general notes

1. Wash the roots.
2. Cover with 10% KOH overnight. The source marks this line with “X”; its meaning is unclear.
3. Heat in a boiling water bath. The source contains an incomplete timing statement and the annotation “45 min”.
4. Rinse with water.
5. Wash with 2% HCl. The source gives both 3 and 4 minutes in an edited line.
6. Do not use water after this step.
7. Transfer to lactophenol containing 0.05% Trypan blue.
8. Heat: the source gives “boil for 5 minutes” and the alternative annotation “15 min at 90 °C”.
9. Rinse with lactic acid or lactophenol without stain.
10. Do not rinse with water, as the source states that this removes the stain.
11. Examine under a stereomicroscope with the roots in a 1:1 glycerol–water mixture.

## Variant 2 — hard, tannin-rich pteridophyte roots

1. Treat with KOH at 90 °C in glass tubes or dishes. Trial times listed: 15, 20 or 30 minutes. KOH concentration is not stated in this variant.
2. Rinse three times with water.
3. Cover with the alkaline solution recorded below.
4. Leave for 10, 20 or 30 minutes; these are trial alternatives in the source.
5. Rinse three or more times with water.
6. Cover with HCl. **Concentration and time missing.**
7. Transfer to 0.05% Trypan blue in lactophenol. **Further conditions missing.**

| Alkaline solution component | Amount in source |
| --- | --- |
| Water | 567 mL |
| 10% H₂O₂ | 30 mL |
| “Ammonium”, described as a floor-cleaning product | 3 mL |

The identity, purity and concentration of the cleaning product are unspecified. This is a transcription of an unresolved formulation.

Source explanation: KOH removes cellular contents; the alkaline solution clears the wall. KOH is also described as being sold as potash flakes.

## Variant 3 — field collection and staining description

Fine roots with a diameter of 2 mm or less were selected in the field and preserved in 70% alcohol for subsequent assessment of mycorrhizal colonization percentage.

Roots were initially cleared with 10% KOH. Because excess tannins hindered clearing, they were subsequently treated with 10% H₂O₂ (Schmidt & Scow, 1986) and rinsed with distilled water.

Roots were then acidified with 1% HCl and stained with cotton blue and lactoglycerol, following Kormanik et al. (1980). Colonization percentage was determined according to “Mc Gonigle…” **[citation incomplete]**.

## Slide preparation and segment recording

1. Place 10 fine-root segments on each slide, with three replicates.
2. Add glycerol gelatin or lactoglycerol and apply a coverslip.
3. Examine under a microscope.
4. Record colonization for each segment on each slide in a spreadsheet.

## Variant 4 — English description from the source

At the end of the experiment, seedling roots were carefully removed from the soil and rinsed with water. Feeder roots were collected from upper, mid and lower portions of the root system. Roots were boiled in 10% KOH (w : v) for 15 min followed by rinsing with deionized water. The roots were then placed in 5% bleach for 2 min followed by a second rinsing with deionized water. The roots were then placed in 5% HCl for 1 min followed by staining in a Trypan blue solution consisting of 950 ml deionized water, 50 ml acetic acid,  glycerol, and  of Trypan blue.

Total AM colonization per cent was quantified using the grid intersect method (Giovannetti & Mosse, 1980) using a dissecting microscope (Olympus SZ40; Olympus, ).
